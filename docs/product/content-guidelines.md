# Content Guidelines

**Status:** Accepted working rules

**Last updated:** 2026-08-24

## Content workflow

1. Write the source content in English.
2. Use verified facts from the canonical candidate profile and evidence banks.
3. Codex reviews the English for clarity, accuracy, and Alexander's natural voice.
4. Alexander performs the final English review.
5. Create the Russian version from the approved meaning, not through literal sentence-by-sentence translation.
6. Publish an item only when both language versions are complete.

## Voice

The website should sound like an experienced product engineer speaking plainly:

- direct;
- calm;
- thoughtful;
- practical;
- technically informed;
- mildly skeptical of hype;
- confident without pretending to know everything.

Prefer the natural reasoning sequence:

1. What happened or what needed to change.
2. Why it mattered.
3. What Alexander decided or built.
4. What changed in practice.
5. What remains uncertain or could be improved, when relevant.

## Writing rules

- Use ordinary international English.
- Prefer concrete verbs, real constraints, and observable behavior.
- Explain technical mechanisms only when they prove judgment or affect the result.
- Use normal paragraphs rather than putting every sentence on a separate line.
- Do not use em dashes in public copy.
- Do not manufacture hooks, lessons, controversy, or calls to action.
- Avoid corporate filler, influencer language, and generic AI-generated portfolio phrasing.
- Do not present a list of technologies as a substitute for a project story.
- Do not make every article or project end in a job-search message.
- Use screenshots, demos, code, diagrams, or measurements as proof rather than decoration.

## Claim rules

- Every material claim needs a source in the content inventory or a direct confirmation from Alexander.
- Do not invent revenue, conversion, retention, time savings, team size, formal leadership, or performance percentages.
- Keep platform scale separate from feature scale.
- Use thousands of inSales clients for the image editor and more than 1,000 clients for abandoned carts.
- Treat GitHub and npm figures as date-sensitive public signals. Reverify them before publication.
- Describe informal mentoring and technical guidance accurately. Do not turn them into people management.
- Distinguish production experience, project experience, and familiarity.
- When evidence is missing, state the gap internally instead of weakening or strengthening the claim arbitrarily.

## Homepage copy constraints

- The first viewport must answer who Alexander is, what kind of problems he solves, and where the visitor can inspect proof.
- Lead with complex product UI and ownership, not availability or a technology cloud.
- Keep the hiring goal primary. Client work remains a secondary path.
- The primary action should lead to selected work. The contact action should remain visible but secondary.
- A portrait may support trust, but project evidence carries the argument.

## Robot test

Before approving public copy, ask:

- Could this paragraph appear unchanged on another developer's portfolio?
- Does it describe a real event, decision, constraint, or result?
- Would Alexander say this sentence aloud?
- Did clarity work introduce corporate language or a perfectly balanced formula that sounds generated?
- Can a reader without inSales context understand it on the first read?
- Is every number and ownership claim supported?

Rewrite anything that fails these checks.
